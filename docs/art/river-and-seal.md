## Nine carp

- Museum record: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/784284
- API: https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/public/collection/v1/objects/784284
- Artist: attributed to Gong Gu, China, 19th century (both uncertain in the record).
- Title: Nine carp; set of four hanging scrolls, ink and color on paper.
- Accession: 2018.147a–d.
- Credit: Purchase, Friends of Asian Art Gifts, 2018.
- Source image: https://images.metmuseum.org/CRDImages/as/web-large/DP-16218-010.jpg
- Museum page marks the photograph Public Domain; API returned `isPublicDomain: true`.
- Open Access / CC0: https://www.metmuseum.org/hubs/open-access
- Local file: `public/assets/history/nine-carp-met-784284.webp`, 555 × 624,
  converted to WebP quality 85, no crop, approximately 45 KB.

The museum interprets the nine fish as wishes for longevity and constancy.
These are Chinese carp, not a claim about Japanese ornamental koi varieties.

## Water and foresight

- Laozi, Dao De Jing, chapter 8: https://ctext.org/dao-de-jing
  (`上善若水`, benefiting things without contending).
- Chapter 64, same source: ordering things before disorder arises.
- Russian text is a short paraphrase; the four-character quotation has our own
  short Russian rendering. The artwork illustrates the river, not Laozi's text.

## Green circular seal: visual interpretation and authority

- Chinese Qing green-and-russet jade bead ornament ending in a small circular
  seal with a chilong finial, diameter 2.3 cm, Christie's:
  https://www.christies.com/lot/lot-a-green-and-russet-jade-bead-ornament-5876681/
- Palace Museum's own explanation of an imperial seal and legitimacy, hosted
  by Google Arts & Culture:
  https://artsandculture.google.com/story/SQXxCJtidl2twA
- Additional material reference: a Chinese seal catalogued as gray-green jade,
  early 20th century, The Met, 19.30.3:
  https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/41011
- Round imperial jade seals also exist: Smithsonian F1978.51a-f, Qianlong,
  ca. 1780. The museum metadata gives the seals' diameters, and a teaching
  catalogue explicitly describes them as round:
  https://www.insea.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/20220511-Hsieh-et-al-TCAC-compressed.pdf

The green circular game icon is an original stylization, not a replica of any
one artifact. Its pale green hue evokes jade; its white characters say
“animal den” (獸穴). Do not call it the historical Heirloom Seal of the Realm,
identify it as a particular emperor's seal, or imply that every jade seal was
imperial. The Christie's example supports green material and circular shape;
the Palace Museum article separately explains imperial authority (its depicted
seal is white and square). The guide labels those two links by their different
subjects to avoid suggesting they depict the same object.

The HKU link was replaced after the user pointed out the visual mismatch with
its square pictured seal. The Taiwan/Cambridge links were previously replaced
after slow loading and certificate problems. The two new pages returned HTTP
200 with normal TLS certificate verification on 2026-10-29. No external seal
photographs have been copied into the website.
